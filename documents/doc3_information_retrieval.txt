Information retrieval is the science of searching for documents, unstructured information within documents, and metadata describing text collections. Classical information retrieval models include the Boolean retrieval model, vector space model, and probabilistic retrieval frameworks. In the vector space model, both user search queries and collection documents are formalized as multi-dimensional term vectors. Term frequency and inverse document frequency, commonly abbreviated as TF-IDF, calculate the statistical weight of each word relative to the document collection. Cosine similarity computes the cosine of the angle between query and document vectors, allowing the search engine to generate a ranked list of relevant results. The inverted index serves as the foundational data structure powering modern retrieval systems. Positional posting lists allow computer algorithms to test phrase proximity, evaluate Boolean operations, and rank documents efficiently. Information retrieval principles provide the mathematical and algorithmic basis for web search engines, digital academic libraries, and automated text indexing.
